## 第一轮：English 原作

### The Box

We were moving on a Saturday. The kind of move where you realize you own too many things and none of them matter very much.

My wife had labeled every box. KITCHEN. BOOKS. BEDROOM. WINTER CLOTHES. Her handwriting in thick black marker. Mine were labeled STUFF and MISC and one that just said JUNK? with a question mark, because I wasn't sure.

By three o'clock we had carried twenty boxes down three flights of stairs. The movers had left. It was just us and the empty apartment, which looked bigger without furniture, and a little sad.

"Last one," I said, dragging a box from the closet. It was smaller than the others, taped shut with yellowing tape. No label.

"What's in there?" she asked.

"No idea."

I cut the tape. Inside, on top, was a stack of old T-shirts I didn't recognize. Under them, a shoebox. Inside the shoebox, a cassette tape.

Not a bought one. A blank one, with a handwritten label: MIX - FOR D - SUMMER '04. My handwriting. From when I was seventeen.

I stared at it.

D was Danielle. My first girlfriend. Summer after high school. We made tapes for each other. You had to sit by the radio and wait for the song, then hit record at exactly the right second. If the DJ talked over the intro, you had to do it again.

I hadn't thought about Danielle in fifteen years. Not properly. She'd gone to college in Toronto. I'd gone to Montreal. We said we'd visit. We didn't.

"What's that?" my wife asked. She was folding blankets.

"Nothing. Just an old tape."

"Can we play it?"

"I don't think we have anything that plays tapes."

She came over and looked. "MIX - FOR D. Who's D?"

I turned the tape over in my hands. The plastic was a little cracked.

"Girl I knew," I said.

"Girlfriend?"

"Yeah."

She nodded. She didn't ask more. She went back to folding.

I sat on the floor with the tape. I tried to remember what was on it. Something by The Strokes, definitely. And that one Coldplay song everyone liked then. And I think — I think I put on that Bright Eyes song she loved, the one she played on repeat when her parents were fighting downstairs.

We used to listen to it in her basement. Her basement had that low ceiling and the old couch that smelled like her dad's cigarettes. We'd sit there and not talk much. Just listen.

The tape had a little piece of paper folded inside the case. I opened it. It said: "D - Don't listen to track 6 alone!! It's ours. - M" And then, in smaller letters: "Call me when you get this."

Track 6. I had no idea what track 6 was. I couldn't remember making this tape at all, and now I was trying to remember a specific song from twenty years ago that 17-year-old me thought was "ours."

My wife was watching me.

"You okay?" she said.

"Yeah."

"You want to keep it?"

I thought about it. The apartment was empty. The tape was small and light and completely useless. I would never own a tape player again. I would never know what track 6 was. And Danielle — last I heard, she was married, living in Vancouver, two kids.

I put the tape back in the shoebox, and the shoebox back in the box.

"Yeah," I said. "I'll keep it."

"Where?"

I looked around the empty living room. Then I put the box by the door, with the others we were taking to the new place.

"With us," I said.

We locked the apartment and left. On the drive to the new house, the tape sat on the back seat. Track 6 stayed silent, which seemed right.

## 第二轮：中译（文学翻译）

### 《盒子》

我们周六搬家，就是那种让你意识到自己东西太多、但哪样好像都没那么重要的搬家。

妻子给每个箱子都贴了标签：厨房、书、卧室、冬衣，黑粗记号笔写的，字很清楚。我的箱子上写的则是：杂物、乱七八糟，还有一个只写了"垃圾？"——带个问号，因为我也不确定。

到三点，我们已经往楼下搬了二十个箱子，三层楼，没电梯。搬家公司的人走了，只剩下我们和空荡荡的屋子，没了家具，屋子显得大了，也有点凄凉。

"最后一个。"我说，从壁橱里拖出一个箱子，比别的都小，封口胶带已经发黄了，没贴标签。

"里面是什么？"她问。

"不知道。"

我划开胶带，最上面是一堆不认识的旧T恤，底下是个鞋盒，鞋盒里有一盘磁带。

不是买的那种，是空白带，手写的标签：MIX - FOR D - 2004年夏天。我的字，十七岁时的字。

我盯着它。

D是丹妮尔，我第一个女朋友，高中毕业那年夏天。我们给彼此做磁带，得守在收音机前等那首歌，掐着秒按录音，要是DJ在前奏上说话，就得重来。

我已经有十五年没好好想起过丹妮尔了。她去了多伦多上大学，我去了蒙特利尔，说好要去看对方，谁也没去。

"那是什么？"妻子问，她在叠毯子。

"没什么，一盘旧磁带。"

"能放吗？"

"咱俩好像没东西能放磁带了。"

她走过来看了一眼："MIX - FOR D，D是谁？"

我把磁带在手里翻来覆去，塑料壳有点裂了。

"以前认识的一个女孩。"我说。

"女朋友？"

"嗯。"

她点点头，没再多问，回去叠毯子了。

我坐在地板上，拿着那盘带，想里面录了些什么。肯定有Strokes的歌，还有那首当时人人都喜欢的Coldplay。我想——我想我还录了那首Bright Eyes，她最爱的那首，爸妈在楼下吵架时她会循环播放的那首。

我们常在她家地下室听，层高很矮，有张旧沙发，一股她爸香烟的味儿。我们就坐在那儿，也不怎么说话，就听着。

磁带盒里折着一张小纸条，我打开，上面写着："D——别一个人听第六首！！那是我们的。——M"，还有一行更小的字："收到给我打电话。"

第六首。我完全想不起第六首是什么了。我连自己做过这盘带都不记得了，现在却在拼命回想二十年前，十七岁的我觉得"是我们的"那首歌到底是哪一首。

妻子看着我。

"没事吧？"她说。

"没事。"

"要留着吗？"

我想了想。屋子已经空了，这盘带又小又轻，完全没用。我这辈子大概都不会再有随身听了，永远也不会知道第六首是什么。而丹妮尔——我最后一次听说，她已经结婚了，在温哥华，两个孩子。

我把磁带放回鞋盒，把鞋盒放回箱子。

"留着。"我说。

"放哪儿？"

我环顾空荡荡的客厅，然后把箱子放在门口，和其他要带去新家的箱子放在一起。

"跟我们走。"我说。

我们锁上门，走了。去新家的路上，磁带坐在后座上。第六首保持沉默，这样也挺好。
