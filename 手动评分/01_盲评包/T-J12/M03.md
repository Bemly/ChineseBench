### 英文原作

**The Tape**

When we moved, there were forty-one boxes. I counted. My wife wrote on each one: KITCHEN, BOOKS, WINTER. The last one had nothing written on it. The tape was yellow.

It was in the back of the closet in the old apartment. I must have packed it eight years ago and forgotten. I cut it open in the new living room, sitting on the floor. No furniture yet. The echo was loud.

On top there was a radio. A small gray radio, the kind with an antenna you pull out. I stared at it a long time before I remembered. It was my father's. He used to listen to baseball on it in the garage. He'd turn it low so my mother wouldn't hear.

Under the radio there was a plastic bag. Inside: batteries, a spare antenna, and a folded piece of paper. A receipt from 2009. Two dollars for batteries at a drugstore that doesn't exist anymore.

I put the batteries in. Nothing. I turned the dial. Nothing. I shook it. Something rattled inside. I opened the back. A dead spider fell out.

My wife came in with coffee. "What's that?" she said.

"My dad's radio."

"Does it work?"

"No."

She sat down beside me. "You want to keep it?"

I didn't answer. I turned the dial again, though I knew it was dead. The sound of the dial was the same. That little click between stations.

My father died in 2016. After the funeral, my mother asked if I wanted anything of his. I said no. I thought I didn't want anything. We weren't close, not in the way people mean. He worked nights. I worked days. We left notes on the fridge.

But I must have taken the radio. I must have packed it that weekend and written nothing on the box, because I didn't know what to call it.

My wife put her hand on my knee. "We can put it on the shelf," she said. "It doesn't have to work."

I nodded. I put the receipt back in the bag. I closed the box and then opened it again, to make sure the radio was still there.

That night we slept on a mattress on the floor. The boxes were all around us. In the dark I could see the outline of that one box. I thought about the garage, the smell of oil, the game turned low. I hadn't thought about it in years. I fell asleep with the radio on the floor beside me, silent.

In the morning I put it on the kitchen shelf, next to the salt. It looked right there. It didn't need to play.

### 中文翻译（只输出译文）

**收音机**

搬家时有四十一个箱子，我数过。我妻子每个都写了字：厨房、书、冬天。最后一个什么都没写，胶带都黄了。

它在旧公寓壁橱最里面。是我八年前打包的，早就忘了。我在新家客厅坐在地板上拆开，还没家具，回声很大。

最上面是一台收音机。灰色小收音机，天线能拉出来的那种。我盯了半天才想起来，是我爸的。他以前在车库里拿它听棒球，开得很小声，怕我妈听见。

收音机下面是个塑料袋，里面是电池、一根备用天线、一张折起来的小票。2009年的，药店买电池，两美元。那家店早没了。

我装上电池，没声。拧旋钮，没声。晃了晃，里面有东西响。打开后盖，掉出一只死蜘蛛。

我妻子拿着咖啡进来：“这是什么？”

“我爸的收音机。”

“还能响吗？”

“不能了。”

她坐到我旁边：“要留着吗？”

我没说话，又拧了一下旋钮，明知是坏的。旋钮的声音还是一样，台与台之间那声轻响。

我爸2016年走的。葬礼后我妈问我要不要留点他的东西，我说不要。我以为我什么都不想要。我们不亲，至少不是人们说的那种亲。他上夜班，我上白班，我们靠冰箱上的纸条联系。

但我一定是拿了这台收音机。那个周末打包的，没写字，因为不知道该叫它什么。

妻子把手放我膝盖上：“放架子上吧，不用能响。”

我点头，把小票放回袋子。把箱子合上，又打开，确认收音机还在。

那晚我们睡在地板的床垫上，箱子围着。黑暗里我看得见那只箱子的轮廓。想起车库、机油味、调小声的比赛。好几年没想起过了。我枕着它睡着了，它放在地板上，安静的。

早上我把它放到厨房架子上，盐旁边。放那儿正好。不用出声。
