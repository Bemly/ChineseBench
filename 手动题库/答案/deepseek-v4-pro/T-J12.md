# T-J12

## 第1轮

The mixtape was at the very bottom of the box, under a stack of yellowed notebooks and one broken calculator. Daniel almost missed it. He was moving in two days and had given himself a single evening to sort through the last of it—the boxes from his old bedroom that had followed him through four apartments and never once been opened.

He picked it up. A cassette, nothing written on the spine, but the front of the case carried a small white label, the glue long since gone brown, with a single word in blue ballpoint: June.

He held it for a long time before the memory came back.

June. The summer after his first year of university. The girl was named Maya, and she drove a car whose cassette player ate tapes whenever the engine got hot, which was always. She made him the mix just before she left for a year abroad. Side A, she told him, was songs she thought he would like. Side B was songs that made her think of him.

He had listened to it exactly once, on the drive home from her going-away party, with the windows down and the highway dark. Then he had tucked it somewhere and, in the careless way of nineteen-year-olds, forgotten it completely. Maya had written letters at first, then postcards, then nothing. The year abroad turned into a permanent move. He had not thought of her in ten years.

Now he sat on the floor of a half-empty room, surrounded by boxes, turning the cassette over and over in his hands. He had no way to play it. His old stereo was gone; his car had a CD player, and after that there had been only a phone. Somewhere in the world there were still machines that could turn this thin ribbon of brown tape back into sound, but there was none in this room.

And yet he found he didn't need to play it. He could remember enough. The way the first track had opened, all acoustic guitar and a voice that cracked on the high notes. The way she had written "play this one loud" next to track six, the ballpoint pressing through the paper. The smell of the car that summer—warm plastic, dust on the dashboard, her shampoo.

He remembered, too, the afternoon she handed it to him. They were sitting on the hood of her car outside the dormitory, and she had pressed the case into his palm as if it were a secret. "Listen to it when you're driving," she had said. "Not at home. It's a driving tape." He had laughed and asked why. She had shrugged and said something he no longer remembered, and then she had kissed him on the cheek, quick and warm, the way you kiss someone you fully expect to see again.

He slipped the cassette into his coat pocket, next to his keys. He would find a way to play it, he decided. He would buy a cheap machine from a secondhand shop if he had to, and he would sit down and listen the way he should have listened twenty years ago: all the way through, without skipping, without turning the volume down at the parts that hurt.

Because some things you keep not because they matter now, but because once, someone made them for you with both hands, and for a little while they were the whole world. And you forgot they were still there, patient as a heartbeat, waiting at the bottom of a box for the day you would finally be ready to hear them again.

## 第2轮

那盘混音磁带在最底下，压在一摞发黄的笔记本和一台坏了的计算器下面。丹尼尔差点就错过了。他还有两天就要搬家，只给自己留了一个晚上来收拾最后这些——那些从他老房间搬出来的箱子，跟着他辗转了四个住处，却一次也没打开过。

他把它拿起来。一盒磁带，盒脊上什么也没写，盒面上贴着一张小标签，胶水早就泛了黄，上面用蓝色圆珠笔写着一个词：六月。

他握着它很久，记忆才慢慢回来。

六月。他大学一年级之后的那个夏天。那个女孩叫玛雅，她有一辆车，那车的卡带机一热就绞带，而它总是热的。临出国交换前，她给他录了这盘带子。A面，她说，是她觉得他会喜欢的歌。B面，是让她想起他的歌。

他只听过一次，在她送别派对后开车回家的路上，车窗摇下来，高速路一片漆黑。然后他就把它随手塞到什么地方，以十九岁那种没心没肺的方式，彻底忘了。玛雅先写信，后来寄明信片，再后来，音讯全无。一年的交换变成了永久定居。他十年没想起过她。

此刻他坐在半空的房间里，四周堆满箱子，把磁带翻来覆去地在手里转。他没东西放它。旧音响早没了；车上是CD机，再往后，就只剩一部手机。这世上总还有机器能把这条细细的褐色磁带重新变回声音，只是不在这个房间里。

可他发现，他并不需要放它。他记得已经足够。第一首歌是怎样开始的，全是木吉他，一个在高音上破掉的声音。她怎样在第六首歌旁边写下"这首开大点声"，圆珠笔把纸都压出了印子。那个夏天车里的味道——晒热的塑料、仪表盘上的灰，还有她的洗发水。

他还记得，她把磁带交给他的那个下午。他们坐在宿舍外她那辆车的引擎盖上，她把盒子按进他掌心，像交付一个秘密。"开车的时候再听，"她说，"别在家听。这是盘开车听的带子。"他笑了，问为什么。她耸耸肩，说了句他现在已经想不起来的话，然后在他脸颊上飞快地、暖暖地亲了一下——就像一个人亲她满心以为还会再见的人那样。

他把磁带滑进外套口袋，贴着钥匙。他决定，一定要想办法放出来。必要的话，就去二手店买台便宜机器，然后坐下来，用二十年前就该有的方式听一遍：从头听到尾，不跳过，也不在那些发疼的地方把音量调小。

因为有些东西你留着，不是因为它们现在还重要，而是因为曾经，有人用两只手为你做好了它，有那么一小段日子，它就是整个世界。而你忘了它一直都在，耐心得像一次心跳，等在箱底，等有一天你终于准备好，再听一遍。
